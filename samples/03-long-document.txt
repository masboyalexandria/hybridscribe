Community Health Post — Monthly Field Report, October (fictional, for demo only).

The Posyandu Melati health post served 214 residents this month, up from 186 in September. The growth came mostly from the new evening hours on Tuesdays, which working parents said they could finally attend. Immunisation coverage for children under five reached 91 percent of the target list. The remaining gap is concentrated in the riverside hamlet, where the footbridge has been closed since the September floods and families must take a forty-minute detour by boat.

Supply notes: the vitamin A stock is sufficient until January. The blood-pressure cuff is damaged and readings drift after about twenty patients, so the midwife has been borrowing the unit from the neighbouring post every Thursday. The refrigerator thermometer recorded two excursions above eight degrees during the power cuts on 9 and 21 October; the vaccines from those days were quarantined and replaced under the district cold-chain protocol, and no compromised doses were administered.

Home visits: the team completed 38 of 45 planned visits. Seven households could not be reached because of the bridge closure. Two toddlers were flagged for follow-up nutrition counselling, and one elderly resident with uncontrolled hypertension was referred to the district clinic. The cadre team asked for a printed referral checklist, because the phone signal at the riverside hamlet is too weak to open the digital form.

Requests for next month: a replacement blood-pressure cuff, printed referral checklists, and a boat-fuel allowance for riverside visits until the bridge reopens. The village office has offered its hall for a Saturday immunisation drive on the 16th, which the team recommends accepting, since Saturday drives historically reach the children of market traders who miss weekday sessions. The monthly coordination meeting is scheduled for the 5th, and the supply request must be submitted to the district office before the 10th.
